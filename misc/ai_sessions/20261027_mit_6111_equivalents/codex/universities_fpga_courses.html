What is included

This is a selection of prominent research universities and specialist engineering institutions with publicly documented FPGA laboratory or practical-course work. It includes all seven institutions requested: МГУ, МИФИ, МФТИ, ИТМО, МЭИ, МИЭТ and МИЭМ (within НИУ ВШЭ), plus KSE’s requested master’s program.

“Top” is interpreted as a focused engineering-education shortlist, not a numerical league table. Inclusion depends on the documented teaching, rather than a general university ranking. The list is not exhaustive; it does not claim that every listed institution ranks above unlisted institutions.

A qualifying course must document FPGA-directed laboratory, practical implementation or project work. A research paper about an FPGA application, a board inventory without a teaching connection, or a digital-logic lecture alone is insufficient. Where only the laboratory work is established, the entry explicitly limits its claims about physical boards or capstones. KSE and the requested school are identified by their actual program type.

Original local-language titles are retained. Russian is also a local official language in Belarus and Kyrgyzstan; published Russian course titles are identified there. Descriptive Armenian and Kazakh translations are marked when the cited course is published in English. Explanatory prose remains in English.

The reference: MIT 6.111

6.111 — Introductory Digital Systems Laboratory, Spring 2006 combines digital logic and state machines, HDL design, simulation, timing and synchronization, laboratory implementation, and a substantial student-designed final project. Its project examples range across audio, video, graphics and other digital systems.

  1. Describe a circuit in an HDL such as Verilog, SystemVerilog or VHDL.
  2. Simulate it and check functional behavior and timing.
  3. Synthesize and implement it for an FPGA.
  4. Demonstrate and debug it on hardware, including external interfaces.
  5. Combine the skills in a substantial design project and explain the results.

The comparison concerns learning activities and outcomes, not formal transfer-credit equivalence. VHDL is a valid counterpart: MIT’s 2002 version used VHDL. For more recent MIT material, see the Fall 2024 6.205 syllabus.

Close curricular match Substantial overlap in the HDL-to-FPGA workflow and project work, as judged from the sources. Partial / evidence-limited match Relevant practical teaching with narrower scope, a different format, or missing public details. Program-level match FPGA laboratory education documented within a broader program.

Course existence and current availability are different findings. Historical syllabi establish what was taught, not what is open for registration today. For example, Caltech’s course is an excellent curricular match, but its official page explicitly says it was not offered in 2025/26. Two semesters, each with 48 contact hours. Students learn VHDL, simulate and implement circuits on a Spartan-3E Starter Kit, and build state machines and interfaces such as UART, a keyboard and an LCD. The second semester is devoted to an original project and its defense.

Cross-university training school Autumn 2026 – Spring 2027

The official school page documents digital-design teaching, FPGA sequential logic, RTL state machines and functional verification. It explicitly labels the displayed detailed curriculum as the previous season’s program, subject to revision.

In-person university clusters provide equipment and local support; remote participants supply their own hardware. Project work with YADRO engineers requires additional selection. How to use the findings

For a full laboratory-course comparison, begin with Berkeley, MSU, MIET, TalTech, RTU and the documented UCLA sequence. Caltech is also a strong curricular model, subject to its offering notice. KSE provides a broader graduate-program route; the Digital Circuit Synthesis School provides a separate practical training route.

When comparing a specific enrollment option, check the current syllabus for mandatory board demonstrations, timing analysis, debugging, HDL requirements and the size of the final project. Course titles and FPGA equipment alone cannot answer those questions. The “close” and “partial” judgments in this report are an interpretation of the cited evidence, not an assessment of teaching quality.

Source access and limitations

University catalogs, syllabi, teaching pages, original teaching publications and organizer announcements were preferred. Stanford’s student project and UCLA’s TA-hosted syllabus are identified as such. Some university systems require a login for full assignments.

KPI’s syllabus was available through indexed official-site text although direct retrieval returned HTTP 403. The BNTU manual and the Tempus report were available through indexed primary-document text; direct PDF access failed during research. BSUIR’s laboratory announcement is accessible through its public Telegram channel linked to the university laboratory. Links are provided for traceability, not as a guarantee of unrestricted access or present enrollment.
