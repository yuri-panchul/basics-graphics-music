Verdict

Both are working designs, and neither has an HDL bug that we could find. Both test suites pass at the pushed commits. Every configuration with TM1638, on both sides, elaborates every lab that builds on any configuration at all. The two board_specific.xdc files are byte for byte the same: both uncomment the same 63 lines of Digilent's file, 62 ports and the clock. What separates them is design choices, the breadboard and the documentation.

Codex is ahead on the lab interface: an internal 8-digit, 8-switch placeholder keeps unmodified labs compiling without TM1638, the buttons go through synchronizers, the split-DIO and HCW132 options of TM1638 are supported, its GPIO test drives every pin both ways, and a script in the repository builds its HTML and its drawings in both languages.

Ours is ahead on the bench: hole-by-hole breadboard layouts for both DACs with the microphone and the regulator plugged in, the regulator Yuri Panchul bought with its pin order taken from their photo, the USB plug off the end of the breadboard, a single 5 V wire and no 5 V rail, a GPIO bus made only of free pins in the order they asked for, and half the clock rate: the heaviest lab we built keeps +8.459 ns of setup slack at our 50 MHz, against +0.693 ns at Codex's 100 MHz.

Two problems in Codex's branch matter most. The first round's 5 V rail beside 24 FPGA pins is still there. And the peripheral pins are now inside the labs' GPIO bus: the guide tells a lab to reclaim them through a lab-local header that the normal Vivado flow does not read, so a lab that does it gets two drivers on a pin. In ours, the configurations without TM1638 still depend on edits to the labs, and three labs on our branch still fail on them; and the script that makes our README HTML lives outside the repository.

  • Two-register synchronizers on the buttons

Problems in Codex's branch

Ranked by severity. Paths are relative to boards/cmod_a7_35_tiny_vga_codex/ of Codex's branch unless they start with labs/, scripts/ or boards/.

Medium

Peripheral pins sit inside the labs' GPIO bus, and the way to reclaim them does not work in the normal build

board_specific_top.sv:202–208, assembly_en.md:197–214

Codex passes all 44 DIP pins to lab_top as gpio[43:0], and nine of them, gpio[21] to gpio[29] on DIP 26–34, are also driven by the TM1638, microphone and DAC instances. The guide says a lab “must leave accessory-owned bits at Z”, and that to reuse them it should leave the interface macro undefined “in a lab-local lab_specific_board_config.svh”.

That second instruction runs into F01, which Codex itself reproduced: the normal flow, scripts/fpga/xilinx_vivado_synthesize.tcl, does not put the lab directory on the include path, so it reads peripherals/lab_specific_board_config.svh, which turns all three interfaces on. A lab that follows the guide keeps the controller's driver and adds its own, two drivers on one pin. Codex's own build driver puts the lab first, so its tests never meet the case. The free pins also come with a gap: a lab that needs more than 21 consecutive pins runs into gpio[21].

Fix: fix F01 first, then check in the top level, at elaboration, that a lab does not drive a pin whose interface is on; or keep peripheral pins out of the bus, as ours does.

Medium

A 5 V rail still runs along 24 FPGA pins

assembly_en.md:138–153, breadboard_dac_en.svg, breadboard_dac_ru.svg

Unchanged since the first round. The left power rail is VU, about 5 V, for the full length of the 30-row breadboard, beside the strips of DIP 1–24. The DIP pins have no series resistors and take 3.75 V at most while the board is powered, so a jumper one hole off puts 5 V on an FPGA pin. Codex feeds the DAC from that rail, which gives the DAC's own regulator headroom: a good reason, but one wire from DIP 24 does the same without a rail.

Fix: run the DAC supply and the regulator input on single wires from the strip of DIP 24, and make both rails 3.3 V and GND.

Medium

The separate-supply option can still feed 3.3 V into an unpowered FPGA

assembly_en.md:58 and assembly_ru.md

Unchanged since the first round. If the rails sag, the guide offers a separate accessory supply with only the grounds shared. With USB unplugged and that supply on, the pull-ups of the TM1638 module hold DIP 26–28 at 3.3 V against an unpowered FPGA, whose pins take 0.55 V at most. The guide still does not state the limit.

Fix: state the limit and switch both supplies together, or drop the option.

Low

100 MHz still leaves the spectrum analyzer under 1 ns of slack

board_specific_top.sv:65–71

Codex clocks everything at 100 MHz so that vga.sv gets its 25 MHz pixel rate as a ÷4 enable. In one flow for both, with the lab's own header, the spectrum analyzer meets timing on Codex's configuration with +0.693 ns of setup slack, against +8.459 ns on ours at 50 MHz, from the same 2,325 LUTs. The microphone monitor, each with its own driver, keeps +1.860 ns against our +7.474 ns.

On both sides the worst path is not logic but the reset register reaching the asynchronous clears of the spectrum filters: no logic between them, and 7.9 ns of routing on Codex's (10.2 ns on ours). Codex's worst logic path is a 12-level carry chain in the filters, with +2.026 ns. Not a bug today, but a student who adds a little logic to these labs will miss timing on Codex's configuration long before ours.

Fix: none needed now. Replicating the reset register, on both sides, would win back most of the reset routing.

Static checks

  • The two board_specific.xdc files are identical, and both are Digilent's file at c8644a9f (SHA-256 56568df3…) with 63 lines uncommented: 62 ports and the clock.
  • TinyVGA bit order, sync polarities and blanking match on both sides: ja = R1, G1, B1, VS, R0, G0, B0, HS.
  • Both reset chains assert at once and release on the third clock edge; Codex's releases after MMCM lock, ours too.
  • Neither design was programmed into a board, and no monitor, audio load or supply was measured.

Merging both

The two branches can live side by side under one cmod_a7_35 menu entry: their directory names differ by the _claude and _codex suffixes, and both suffixes are in fpga_board_base_name. Three files conflict:

  • boards/README.csv: one row for the board, cmod_a7_35, as ours, with its TM1638 column “either”;
  • scripts/steps/00_setup.source_bash: both add suffixes and Xilinx toolchain names; our glob cmod_a7* covers Codex's four names, and the banner line Codex put at its top would sit over a file it edited in eight lines;
  • scripts/tests/test_board_menu.py: Codex's removesuffix change is not needed with a cmod_a7_35 row.

The pin plans are incompatible: a bitstream from one branch needs that branch's wiring. The eight lab fixes on our branch help Codex's configurations too (87 of 89 labs instead of 84 of 88) and any other board with one digit or two LEDs.
