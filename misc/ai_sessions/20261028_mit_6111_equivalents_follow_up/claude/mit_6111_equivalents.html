The reference course

6.2056.111

Digital Systems Laboratory I

MIT renumbered 6.111 (Introductory Digital Systems Laboratory) as 6.205 in its new numbering.

  • Digital design on FPGAs in Verilog, simulated with iVerilog and built with AMD Vivado.
  • Each student borrows a Real Digital Urbana board for the term.
  • The first half is lectures and about eight weekly labs and exercises (48% of the grade). The second half is a large final project (52%). There are no exams.
  • 12 units (1-5-6). The prerequisite is 6.191, formerly 6.004.

How the regions compare

What the searches show about each region. It reflects what universities publish online, which varies a lot from region to region, as well as what they teach.

California

8 of 17 close

FPGA labs are part of the first or second digital design course at nearly every school checked, from Berkeley and Stanford to the Cal State campuses. The closest equivalents pair HDL labs with a large project of the student's choice: Berkeley 151LB, Harvey Mudd E155 (labs and project 45% each), Caltech 119, USC 354L and UC Santa Cruz 125. Catalogs are public and current but seldom name a board. Those named are Digilent boards (Basys 3, Nexys4, PYNQ) and Terasic boards (DE0-CV, DE1, DE10-Lite, DE2-115), plus Berkeley's Zynq UltraScale+ AUP-ZU3 and Harvey Mudd's own iCE40 board.

Russia

7 of 28 close

The widest spread of FPGA teaching, with the most current materials in Moscow: MIET's open RISC-V course book, MIPT's pipelined RISC-V course, MPEI's lab manual with Icarus Verilog and Cyclone IV, and MIEM's SoC courses. Regional universities such as those in Voronezh, Novosibirsk, Tomsk and Nizhny Novgorod have FPGA courses whose public syllabi mostly date from 2016–2022, often in VHDL on older boards (Spartan-3, Cyclone II); several include a course project built on the board. The boards named are mostly Terasic DE-series and Digilent Nexys, alongside cheaper Chinese and Russian boards (OMDAZZ at MPEI, Marsohod2 and LESO2 at SibSUTIS) and old Xilinx Starter Kits. YADRO's free Synthesis School, at 26 sites, is the closest thing to a nationwide 6.111. DonNTU, in the Russian-controlled part of Ukraine's Donetsk region, describes FPGA work but publishes no syllabus.

Belarus

1 of 5 close

Concentrated in two places: BSUIR in Minsk, with its YADRO lab and a two-semester FPGA course, and Gomel, with two Synthesis School clusters. BNTU has a 2013 VHDL lab practicum in Xilinx ISE that ends with a PicoBlaze system. The other universities publish little at course level, and the only board named is a Basys 3 in a BNTU conference paper.

Ukraine

1 of 7 close

Detailed public syllabi at KPI, KNU and Odesa Polytechnic show FPGA labs on Altera-era hardware in Quartus II (Terasic DE boards, ACEX boards, a DIGIASIC stand), built around DSP and interfaces rather than open-ended projects. KSE's new master's program, run with ETH Zürich on Zybo Z7 boards, is the most current and the closest match.

Other former Soviet republics

1 of 33 close, 19 not found

The Baltic states publish current course records in ECTS, often in English as well: the University of Latvia's DIP course builds pipelined CPUs and ends in projects, and TalTech and RTU teach SystemVerilog or VHDL with mini-projects or individual projects. Elsewhere there are one to three universities with a course per country in Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Moldova and Georgia, and none were found in Uzbekistan, Tajikistan or Turkmenistan. Armenia's chip-design teaching runs mainly through Synopsys and ASIC tools rather than FPGAs; its FPGA activity shows up in events such as the 2025 EDA Connect hackathon on Gowin boards, and in Kyrgyzstan in AUCA's 2022 Siemens EDA seminar. Boards are named only at the University of Latvia (Spartan-3E, Anvyl, PYNQ-Z1), TalTech (Nexys 4 DDR) and ADA University (ALINX AX4010).

Across all regions

  • The strongest matches usually have an industry or university partner behind them: YADRO in Russia and Belarus, ETH Zürich at KSE, Synopsys in Armenia. The other strong ones are long-running courses with open materials, like MIET's and Berkeley's.
  • Building a processor, usually RISC-V, is the typical capstone of the strongest courses: MIET, MIPT, SibSUTIS, the University of Latvia, Berkeley and Harvey Mudd's E85.
  • Two board families dominate everywhere: Terasic boards with Intel (Altera) FPGAs and Digilent boards with AMD (Xilinx) FPGAs. Cheaper boards appear too: Chinese Cyclone IV boards such as OMDAZZ at MPEI, Russian Marsohod and LESO boards at SibSUTIS, and Gowin Tang boards on the Synthesis School's list. Among these universities only Harvey Mudd teaches on Lattice. Most sources don't name a board at all, so the board column is often empty.
  • Public evidence thins out sharply away from Moscow, St. Petersburg and the Baltic capitals, so “Not found” in Central Asia and the Caucasus says more about what's online than about what's taught.

Microprocessor-based Systems

E155

Fall 2026 syllabus. Students build systems from a Lattice FPGA (programmed in Lattice Radiant), a microcontroller and other chips. They work on the course's own development board, which carries an UPduino v3.1 (Lattice iCE40 UP5K) and an STM32 Nucleo, from a $75 kit. Seven labs, then a project of their own choice that must make real use of both the FPGA and the MCU, with a proposal, design review, midpoint and final checkoffs, and a report. Labs and the project are each 45% of the grade, close to 6.205's split.

How this was checked

  • Page checked means I opened the course page or PDF on 27 or 28 October 2026 and the details in the row come from it.
  • From search results means the page was blocked, timed out or had moved when I tried it from here, so the details come from the search engine's copy of the page. Open the link before relying on them.
  • Not found means searches in English and the local language, usually limited to the university's own site, turned up no FPGA or HDL course page. It isn't proof the university has no such course.
  • The match level is my judgment from the course description, measured against 6.205: FPGA labs in an HDL plus a substantial build.
  • On 27 October 2026 the findings were checked against a second report on the same question, prepared by Codex, and updated where its sources held up. The comparison lists what changed and where the two reports disagree.
  • On 28 October 2026 Codex's expanded report was checked the same way. It led to corrections (SF State's outline date, UC Davis's lab hours, which MIPT course uses the Zynq board, the source of MIEM's board list), to new sources (BNTU's 2013 lab practicum, KRSU's 2012 Tempus report and 2024 plan, a 2021 VILNIUS TECH article, UCSD's 2014 page, Samara's 2026 cluster announcement) and to the Agricultural University of Georgia. Its report that the NSU syllabus link is dead didn't hold: the PDF loads. Its sources for Nazarbayev's ECE 482 and for TUSUR's lab manuals didn't load from here, so they aren't used. The comparison page above still describes the 27 October versions.
  • The FPGA board column gives the board a course page, lab handout, course repository or paper names, with the year when the source is old. “Not named” means none of the sources I read names one.
  • The DonNTU site (donntu.ru) doesn't resolve from here, so its row was checked against Web Archive copies; the row links both the live page and the copies.
  • Course codes, credits and boards change from year to year. Several entries (MSU's VMK elective, the MIEM courses, the USC catalogue entry) are from earlier years.
