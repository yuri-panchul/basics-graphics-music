What should come after the first FPGA course?

An FPGA class is a starting point. It does not tell us whether students can build a non-CPU pipeline, handle backpressure, verify corner cases or explain negative slack in an ASIC timing report.

I looked beyond course titles: 49 criteria, seven areas, and 92 university/program entries. The central distinction is between mentioning a technique and giving students a task that demonstrates it.

Three questions for a curriculum review: Can students connect FIFOs and pipelines under stalls? Can they verify transactions and measure functional coverage? Can they interpret an ASIC timing report and justify a design change?

The report links every awarded point to evidence. Unverified means unknown—not that a university does not teach the subject. Student projects, optional courses and historical materials are labeled.
