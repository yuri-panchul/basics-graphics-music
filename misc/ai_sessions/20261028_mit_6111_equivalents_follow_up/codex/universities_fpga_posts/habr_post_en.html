49 checks for an HDL/FPGA curriculum

An FPGA class is a starting point. It does not tell us whether students can build a non-CPU pipeline, handle backpressure, verify corner cases or explain negative slack in an ASIC timing report.

The report asks for concrete evidence: pipeline plus FIFO behavior under backpressure; transaction checking; constrained randomization and functional coverage; assertions in simulation and formal tools; ASIC timing/power/floorplanning; student-built CPU RTL; application blocks; and student-written EDA algorithms.

The expanded survey contains 92 assessed entries: 90 universities and two separately identified cross-university programs. It retains the California/former-USSR survey and adds 20 comparison entries, including MIT, Princeton, Michigan, NC State, Portland State, Georgia Tech, ETH, UCL, Cambridge and selected Asian institutions.

Careful exclusions matter as much as positive findings. A proposed multicore course is not an established class. A processor simulator written in C is not processor RTL. A research paper about an accelerator is not evidence of a teaching assignment. Hosting the Digital Circuit Synthesis School does not give a university all of the School’s points.

The 49-item rubric gives one point per documented topic across microarchitecture, clocks/memory/gating, verification, ASIC implementation, processor RTL, applications and EDA software. A question mark means unverified, not absent. Scores describe the inspected public evidence, not the quality of a university or its graduates.
