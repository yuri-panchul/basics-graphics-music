12

24-bit DVI Pmod

Add-on
Adds
DVI video on an HDMI connector. Silkscreen: “iCEBreaker PMOD” and “24bit DVI”.
Connects
Double Pmod on PMOD1A and PMOD1B, with pins D0–D11, CK, DE, HS and VS. Twelve data lines carry 24-bit colour by sending 12 bits on each clock edge. The transmitter chip is a 64-pin TQFP whose marking is unreadable.
BGM
DVI_24B, 8 bits per colour, is the default in the icebreaker_dvi_24b_* folders; DVI_12B gives 4 bits per colour
13

TM1638 “LED&KEY” module

Add-on
Adds
Eight 7-segment digits, eight LEDs and eight buttons behind one TM1638 driver chip
Connects
Jumper wires to PMOD2: data on P2_8, clock on P2_9 and strobe on P2_10, as in icebreaker_dvi_24b_tm1638_yosys/board_specific_top.sv
BGM
The labs use it for digits, LEDs and keys on boards that lack them, in every *_tm1638_* folder

What the photos can't settle

  • OrangeCrab, 25F or 85F. Peel back or read around the QR label on the ECP5. BGM's folder is built for the 25F.
  • STEP board, MachXO2 or MAX10. Read the FPGA marking. A MAX10 board would belong with the Intel boards and Quartus.
  • iCESugar-nano FPGA. Check whether the chip marked “Lattice” is the LP1K in its 2.5 mm package or a larger part.
  • iCEBreaker revision. Move the jumper wires and read the “V1.x” label.
  • Colour depth of the two VGA add-ons. The photos show the resistor DACs but not how many bits per colour they take. BGM drives the iCE40-IO with 4 bits per colour.
