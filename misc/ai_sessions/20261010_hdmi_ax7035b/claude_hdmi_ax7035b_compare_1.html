Two TMDS Transmitters

Both branches put 640×480 DVI video out of the AX7035B's HDMI1 connector using OSERDESE2 serializers and the repository's shared tmds_encoder. They disagree about where the lab's clock comes from, where the pipeline register goes, and what gets written down. Everything below that is measured was measured on this machine with Vivado 2026.1.

The split is the main structural difference. Mine is two modules that know nothing about this board — a 7-series clock generator and a 7-series DVI transmitter — wired together in the board top, which keeps using the vga timing generator that about thirty other boards use. Theirs is one module named after the board that also hands the lab its clock, its reset and its pixel coordinates, which makes the board top shorter and the module unusable anywhere else without renaming.

Where we converged

Independently, and in a few cases after the same mistake

  • The constraints file. Both branches now ship the vendor's own HDMI block with the comment marks removed, plus one added line. Both of us first rewrote that block with the negative pins spelled out, and both of us then reverted it — theirs in a67af903 "Restored the original XDC file", mine in 3d1554a2. The two files differ only in where set_false_path -from [get_ports rst_n] sits and what its comment says.
  • Serializer topology. Master/slave OSERDESE2 pair per lane, DATA_WIDTH 10, DDR, LSB first, bits 8 and 9 into the slave's D3/D4, SHIFTOUT1/2 to SHIFTIN1/2, OBUFDS with IOSTANDARD TMDS_33 and SLEW FAST. Eight OSERDESE2 in both builds.
  • The clock lane. Both send the pixel clock as the constant symbol 10'b1111100000 through a fourth identical serializer, so its latency matches the data lanes.
  • Clocking arithmetic. Both multiply the 50 MHz board clock to a 1000 MHz VCO and divide it down to 125 MHz serial and 25 MHz pixel, from a single MMCME2_BASE.
  • Reset discipline. Both assert reset asynchronously on the button or on loss of lock and release it synchronously with the pixel clock, which is CLKDIV of the serializer, so the master and slave of a pair cannot frame a symbol differently.
  • The encoder. Both instantiate the repository's tmds_encoder from peripherals/dvi.sv rather than copying one. (Mine only after deleting a copy of boards/marsohod3gw2/svo_tmds.v that the first version had carried in.)

Why their slack is better

The two worst paths say it exactly:

mine   i_vga/hpos_reg[2]/C → i_hdmi_tx/channel[0].data_channel.i_tmds_encoder/cnt_reg[4]/D   22.962 ns
theirs   i_hdmi/i_sync/pixel_y_o_reg[5]/C → i_hdmi/rgb_reg[2][5]/D   29.441 ns

In my design one pixel clock has to cover the coordinate register, the whole of the lab's combinational graphics logic, and the TMDS encoder's disparity arithmetic. In theirs the lab's logic ends at a register, and the encoder starts the next cycle with a fresh 40 ns. At 25 MHz neither is close to failing; the margin is what you would spend on a higher pixel clock or a heavier lab. Their register costs 27 flip-flops and changes nothing about alignment, because they delay the colours and the sync signals by the same one cycle.

One honest caveat about these numbers

2_1_rectangle_ellipse_parabola drives its colours from always_comb and never uses clk, rst or slow_clk. In their design the lab's 50 MHz clock therefore has no surviving load and Vivado removes it: the routed clock report lists three global clocks (pixel 132 loads, serial 8, feedback 1) and no 50 MHz net. So this build does not exercise their second clock domain at all, and the LUT, register and BUFG columns understate what their architecture costs on a lab that does use the lab clock. Mine has two global clocks (pixel 85 loads, serial 8) and that does not change with the lab.

Pros and cons

After the measurements, not before them

Mine

In favour

  • Tested on the board. It drives a real monitor. Theirs has never been on hardware — their own README says so.
  • Reusable pieces. hdmi_clk_gen and hdmi_tx are 7-series modules with no AX7035B in them; the next Artix board needs a port list, not a transmitter.
  • Clock numbers come from parameters. clk_mhz and pixel_mhz actually drive the MMCM dividers, so a different board clock is a parameter change rather than an edit.
  • One clock domain for lab and video. No crossing to reason about, one reset synchronizer, two BUFGs, 40 ns for every lab path.
  • Follows the repository's video convention — the shared vga generator and the x10/y10 wiring that nexys_a7_100, basys3 and the rest use, including N_MIXER_PIPE_STAGES as a documented alignment knob.
  • Smaller: 320 LUT / 77 FF / 2 BUFG against 342 / 122 / 3.

Against

  • No testbench at all. Four bugs were found by comparing against their code and by looking at a dark monitor, when three of them would have failed a simulation in seconds.
  • No document. Nothing records the video mode, the pin table, the connector, or what is not implemented.
  • 6.5 ns less slack, because the lab's logic and the encoder share one pixel clock.
  • Lab at 25 MHz — half the throughput of the board clock, and a deviation from every other HDMI board in the repository (F3).
  • MMCM feedback unbuffered while the primitive's compensation assumes a BUFG (F4).
  • Two comment claims were wrong and one cited a document I had not read (F1, F2, F3).

Theirs

In favour

  • A real testbench. It decodes the differential output with AMD's own MMCM, OSERDESE2 and OBUFDS models, checks every pixel of a complete frame against a coordinate-derived pattern, checks the control symbols in blanking, checks that the forwarded clock is five low and five high bits, and then does it again after an asynchronous reset.
  • A real README: the mode and its 59.52 Hz, the full timing table, the pin table with both halves of each pair, how to run it, and an explicit list of what is missing — EDID, hot-plug, audio, HDCP.
  • More slack, measured, from a register that costs 27 flip-flops.
  • Lab clock stays at the board's 50 MHz, which is what a7_lite_35t and the Gowin HDMI boards do.
  • MMCM feedback through a BUFG, which is what AMD's own clocking wizard emits — there is a generated instance of exactly that in this repository.
  • The reset and the enable read directly: assign hdmi_enable = ~ pixel_rst; is better than my three-term conditional on a localparam that is always 1.
  • Honest about its limits in writing, including that nothing has been tried on hardware.

Against

  • Board-specific by construction. One module does clocking, timing, encoding, serialization and the lab's clock and reset; nothing in it is reusable for the next Xilinx board.
  • Clocking is hardcoded: CLKIN1_PERIOD 20.0, CLKFBOUT_MULT_F 20.0 and the three dividers are literals, while the module still receives none of clk_mhz, pixel_mhz, screen_width or screen_height. Their README admits the parameters do not select a mode (F7).
  • Coordinate widths are literals — output [9:0] x, output [8:0] y — decoupled from w_x and w_y in the top.
  • Two clock domains mean every lab path that touches x or y is timed across 50 and 25 MHz, not within one 40 ns pixel (F9).
  • The testbench reaches into the DUT (dut.serial_clk) and is written against this module's exact ports, so it does not transfer.
  • Two claims about pin M6 are still in their tree, one of them in the README (F1, F2).

Findings

Eleven, with the evidence for each

F1"Active high despite the name" is not a finding, it is a misreadingdefectboth

OEN expands to output enable. The N is the last letter of "enable", not an active-low bar, so active high is the plain reading of the vendor's name and there is nothing to be surprised by. Both of us drove the pin low to enable it in our first version, both of us then wrote the fix up as the vendor's name being misleading. It is still in their tree twice.

theirs, ax7035b_hdmi.sv   // M6 controls HDMI1's +5 V supply and is active high, despite OEN's name.
theirs, README.md        It is **active high**, despite the existing signal name.
mine                       corrected in 3d1554a2; the comment now just says driving it high turns the supply on

Worth fixing in their two files. The polarity itself is right.

F2The ALINX manual is cited as the source of the pins and the polaritydefectmine, and to confirm on theirs

My comment and the message of fd740412 both said the polarity was "taken from the HDMI section of the ALINX AX7035B user manual", with a URL. I never opened that document. The polarity came from the monitor staying dark with the pin low, from their implementation driving it high, and from the board then working. The citation is removed from my tree; the commit message stays as it is, with the correction recorded in 3d1554a2.

theirs, README.md   Pin assignments and power-enable polarity follow the
                   [ALINX AX7035B user manual, HDMI section](https://alinx.com/.../AX7035B_UG.pdf)
theirs, 4b2fe932    # HDMI1 output (J6), ALINX AX7035B manual, section 9.

Their citation may well be real — I cannot tell from here, and it should be confirmed rather than assumed. What is certain is that the naming argument attached to it is wrong either way, and that the pins did not need a manual: they were already in the vendor's constraints file, commented out.

F3My comment claims the other HDMI boards clock the lab from the pixel clock. None of them do.defectmine

The comment above my clock generator reads "The lab itself runs on the pixel clock, as it does on the other HDMI boards." That is false for every HDMI board in the repository, and the closest precedent is the clearest case:

boards/a7_lite_35t           clk_wiz: 250 MHz serial, 50 MHz lab, 25 MHz pixel → lab at 50
boards/tang_nano_9k_hdmi_tm1638   wire clk = CLK; → lab at the 27 MHz board clock
boards/tang_nano_20k_hdmi_tm1638  wire clk = CLK; → lab at 27 MHz
boards/marsohod3gw2                 clk_mhz = 100, video on its own pixel clock

Their choice is the conventional one and mine is the outlier. The design still works — the lab receives lab_mhz = 25 as its clk_mhz, so every derived timer scales — but the comment asserts a convention that does not exist, which is the same failure as F1 and F2 in a third place. The comment has to go whether or not the clocking changes.

F4My MMCM feedback skips the BUFG that its compensation mode assumesriskmine

I tie CLKFBOUT straight to CLKFBIN. They put a BUFG in between, which looked like a wasted global buffer until I checked what the primitive does: MMCME2_BASE has no COMPENSATION parameter, it wraps MMCME2_ADV without passing one, and that defaults to ZHOLD — the mode that models a BUFG delay in the feedback path. AMD's own clocking wizard emits exactly their arrangement, and there is a generated instance of it in this repository:

unisims/MMCME2_BASE.v           MMCME2_ADV #( ... ) mmcm_adv_1 // no COMPENSATION passed
unisims/MMCME2_ADV.v           parameter COMPENSATION = "ZHOLD",
boards/a7_lite_35t/clk_wiz_clk_wiz.v  .COMPENSATION ("ZHOLD") ... BUFG clkf_buf (.O (clkfbout_buf_clk_wiz), .I (clkfbout_clk_wiz));

The consequence here is a constant phase offset between sys_clk and every MMCM output, which nothing in this design measures: sys_clk feeds only the MMCM, and rst_n is on a false path. Vivado raised no warning and the build is clean. So this is not a bug in the picture, but theirs is the template-correct version and mine should adopt it — one BUFG out of thirty-two.

F5My enable expression says ~rst the long wayriskmine
mine    localparam hdmi_output_enable = 1'b1;
       assign HDMI_OEN = rst ? ~ hdmi_output_enable : hdmi_output_enable;
theirs   assign hdmi_enable = ~ pixel_rst;

The localparam is always 1, so the conditional is ~rst and the non-graphics branch's ~hdmi_output_enable is 1'b0. The indirection existed to carry the "despite the name" explanation that F1 just removed, so it has lost its last reason to exist.

F6Mine has no simulation, and three of the four bugs it shipped were simulatabledefectmine

The first version of my branch built cleanly, met timing, and produced no picture. The four causes were the inverted enable, an asynchronously released serializer reset, a copied TMDS encoder, and a clock-lane symbol in the wrong phase. Against their testbench:

  • Inverted enable — caught. The testbench holds its state machine idle while enable is low, so no frame would ever be decoded and the 70 ms watchdog would fire.
  • Clock lane inverted — caught, explicitly: it finds bit zero on the forwarded clock's falling edge and then asserts cp !== (bit_number >= 5) on every bit.
  • Reset released asynchronously — probably caught: the second half of the test re-decodes a whole frame after an asynchronous reset, which is where misframed master/slave halves show up.
  • Duplicated encoder — not caught. It was duplication, not misbehaviour.

A testbench is the single largest thing their branch has and mine does not.

F7Their clocking and coordinate widths are literals inside a module with parameters passed around itrisktheirs

ax7035b_hdmi takes no parameters. CLKIN1_PERIOD 20.0, CLKFBOUT_MULT_F 20.0, CLKOUT0_DIVIDE_F 8.0, CLKOUT1_DIVIDE 40 and CLKOUT2_DIVIDE 20 are written out, and x and y are [9:0] and [8:0]. Meanwhile the board top still declares clk_mhz, pixel_mhz, screen_width, screen_height, w_x and w_y and passes none of them in. Change the board clock or the screen and the design is quietly wrong rather than loudly broken. Their README says as much, which is the right mitigation but not a fix.

Mine derives the five MMCM numbers from clk_mhz and pixel_mhz — though only within the fixed 1000 MHz VCO, and the screen timing still comes from vga's own constants, so mine is parameterized in the clocking and fixed in the mode.

F8Their lab clock is a second domain that this comparison never exercisedrisktheirs

Four BUFGs are instantiated — feedback, pixel, serial, lab — and three survive, because 2_1 never uses the lab clock. On a lab that does, their design pays the fourth BUFG and puts every path between the lab and the pixel domain on a 20 ns check instead of a 40 ns one. The crossing is legal and timed, since both clocks come from one MMCM; it is simply tighter, and it is the part of their architecture that no measurement here covers.

F9Neither constrains the TMDS output timingacceptedboth

There is no set_output_delay, no create_generated_clock for the forwarded clock, and no skew budget between the four lanes in either XDC. Both rely on the serializers being identical and in the same bank, which they are — all four pairs are in bank 35. At a 250 Mbit/s lane rate that is ordinary practice and both builds route with hold slack to spare (0.130 ns, inside the serializer, in both). Their README states the limitation; mine does not state it anywhere.

F10Checked and not a defect: my coordinate truncation and their clamped coordinatesclearedboth

I wire vga's 10-bit vpos into a 9-bit y, so during the last thirteen blanking lines the lab sees a wrapped row number. That is the x10/y10 idiom copied verbatim from nexys_a7_100 and basys3 and it is harmless because display_on is low there. Theirs sees dvi_sync's coordinates, which are clamped to zero outside the visible window instead. Different, both fine.

Pixel alignment is also fine in both, for opposite-looking reasons: every lab in labs/2_graphics drives its colours from always_comb, so my undelayed sync signals line up with the lab's colours, and their one-cycle delay of colours and syncs together preserves the same alignment.

F11Upstream: dvi_top delays its sync signals for a colour path the graphics labs do not haveunverifiedrepository

Neither branch uses dvi_top, but a7_lite_35t and the Gowin HDMI boards do, and it contains this:

peripherals/dvi.sv   // Delay hsync/vsync and visible_range by 1 clock cycle to account for
                     // color delay in image_gen

The colours are not delayed with them. If the colour source is combinational — which every labs/2_graphics lab is — the colours then lead the sync signals and the DE window by one pixel. The shared vga generator has N_MIXER_PIPE_STAGES for exactly this adjustment and dvi_sync has no equivalent. I have not put a camera on an a7_lite_35t, so this is a suspicion from reading, not a confirmed one-pixel shift, and it belongs upstream rather than in either branch.

What each side should take

Concrete, in the order I would do it

Mine, from theirs

  • The testbench, adapted to hdmi_tx's ports — the decoder, the full-frame check, the forwarded-clock check and the reset-recovery pass are all independent of their module structure once the hierarchical dut.serial_clk reference is replaced by the testbench's own serial clock.
  • A board README with the mode, the timing table, the pin table, how to run it, and what is not implemented.
  • The colour and sync register stage before the encoder: 27 flip-flops for a measured 6.479 ns.
  • The BUFG in the MMCM feedback (F4).
  • assign HDMI_OEN = ~ rst; in place of my conditional (F5).
  • The lab clock at the board's 50 MHz, which is both the repository's convention and twice the throughput, at the cost of the second reset synchronizer they already wrote (F3).
  • set_false_path next to the rst_n pin rather than at the end of the HDMI block — it is a constraint about that port.

Theirs, from mine

  • Split the module. A 7-series clock generator and a 7-series DVI transmitter with no board name in them, so a7_lite_35t and the next Artix board can use the same two files. Their ax7035b_tmds_lane is already exactly that, one rename away.
  • Feed the parameters through to the MMCM dividers and the coordinate widths (F7), instead of documenting that they do nothing.
  • Fix the two M6 claims in ax7035b_hdmi.sv and README.md (F1), and confirm or drop the manual citation (F2).
  • Put it on the board. Everything else in their branch is better documented and better verified than mine except this, and it is the one that found four bugs in mine.
  • Consider the single-domain option for the lab clock, which removes the crossing entirely — not as a replacement for their design, but as the thing to compare against on a lab that actually uses the lab clock (F8).

One tempting idea that does not work: moving a shared Xilinx transmitter into peripherals/. scripts/fpga/xilinx_vivado_synthesize.tcl reads peripherals/*.sv for every board, and the Quartus, Gowin, Efinity and yosys flows read the same directory, so a file containing OSERDESE2 and OBUFDS would be handed to toolchains that have never heard of them. Sharing it needs a new home that only the Xilinx flow reads — which is why a7_lite_35t keeps its generated clk_wiz in its own board directory, and why both of our transmitters sit in boards/alinx_ax7035b/.

Open checks

What I did not establish, and how to establish it

Does their testbench pass, here, now?

Yes — reproduced here. Their documented commands ran unchanged against their worktree with Vivado 2026.1: xvlog analysed all four files and xelab built the snapshot against unisims_ver and secureip with no errors, binding the real MMCME2_BASE, two OSERDESE2 configurations and OBUFDS. xsim -runall then printed all three of its checks and exited 0:

PASS: frame 1, RGB, sync and serialization
PASS: frame 2, RGB, sync and serialization
PASS: HDMI reset recovery
$finish called at time : 64963959 ns

It is not cheap — 64.96 ms of simulated time, 34 minutes of CPU and a 423 MB peak on this host — but it is exactly the check my branch does not have, and it passes

Does their branch actually produce a picture?

Their README reports simulation and timing, and states plainly that nothing has been tried on hardware. Build their branch for a graphics lab and plug in a monitor. Mine was dark on the first try while meeting timing, which is precisely why this check cannot be skipped.

What do the two designs cost on a lab that uses the lab clock?

Everything measured above used a combinational lab, which deletes their second clock domain. Rebuild both for 2_2_rectangle_ellipse_parabola_moving or 2_10_color_shapes_and_functions and compare the BUFG count, the register count and the worst path between the lab and pixel domains.

Did anyone read the ALINX manual?

I did not, and I said I had. Open the AX7035B manual's HDMI section and the schematic, and confirm three things on paper: that M6 drives the connector's 5 V switch, its polarity, and that the four pairs belong to HDMI1 / J6 rather than the second connector. Then whoever cites it has actually read it.

Is there a one-pixel shift on the boards that use dvi_top?

F11 is a reading, not a measurement. Photograph a sharp vertical edge from 2_3_color_stripes on an a7_lite_35t, or simulate dvi_top with a combinational colour source and compare the first visible symbol's position against dvi_sync's own visible_range_o.

Does the monitor need anything we are not giving it?

Neither design reads hot-plug detect or EDID, and neither sends HDMI data islands, so the output is DVI over an HDMI connector at a non-standard 25 MHz (59.52 Hz instead of 60). That works on the monitor mine was tested with. Try two or three more displays, especially one that only advertises 60 Hz modes, before calling the mode safe.

Recommendation

One implementation, not two

Neither branch should be merged as it stands, and the merge is not a close call on most of the files. Their verification artifacts and their clocking discipline are better; my module boundaries and parameterization are better; the hardware evidence is only on my side.

  1. Keep my two-file split — hdmi_clk_gen.sv and hdmi_tx.sv, with no board name in either — as the shape of the thing, so the second Xilinx HDMI board is cheap.
  2. Take their register stage into hdmi_tx: register the colours and the sync signals together on the pixel clock before the encoders. Measured 6.479 ns, no alignment change.
  3. Take their clocking choices into hdmi_clk_gen: a BUFG in the feedback path, and a third output at the board clock so the lab keeps its 50 MHz with its own reset synchronizer. The module then produces exactly what a7_lite_35t's clk_wiz produces, from a parameterized primitive instead of generated IP.
  4. Port their testbench against the new hdmi_tx and keep it in boards/alinx_ax7035b/tests/, which is where they put it.
  5. Take their README, with the two M6 sentences rewritten and the manual citation either verified or dropped.
  6. Keep both XDCs — they are already the same file, and the right one: the vendor's own constraints with the comment marks removed, plus set_false_path on rst_n.
  7. Then put it on the board again, because every one of these steps touches the datapath or the clocking, and a clean build has already proved once that it means nothing.

The honest summary of the last two days on this board: their branch found four bugs in mine by being written independently, and their testbench would have found three of them without anyone looking at a monitor. The two errors I corrected in my own tree this evening — a misread signal name and a document I cited without reading — are both still in theirs, which is the symmetry worth noticing. Neither of us checked the vendor's manual; one of us said he had.
