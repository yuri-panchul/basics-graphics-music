Two BRS-100 Ports

Both branches add four configurations for the same board, adapter and modules: 480×272 and 800×480, each with the manufacturer's .cst and with a function-named _alt one. They differ in pin placement, clocking, how they treat the vendor file, how they power the TM1638, and whether the free Gowin edition can build them at all. Everything marked measured was measured on this machine with Gowin V1.9.9 Beta-4 Education; nothing has run on a real board.

Where we converged

Independent choices that came out the same

  • The adapter is MuseLab's PMOD-RGBLCD v1.3; its J2 is a 2×15 header numbered 1 opposite 30, not a 12-pin Pmod; R0/G0/B0 are the low bits; SB1–SB4 must stay open; J2.23 is 5 V through R4 and only feeds the backlight boost.
  • LCD 20 pins, TM1638 3, DAC 3 (SCK tied to ground), both resolutions with identical jumpers.
  • The vendor .cst names the header pad_io[1..32], J5 then J6; the top module declares them inout [32:1].
  • Gowin rejects the vendor's DRIVE=8 on a pin used only as input, the microphone data; we found it independently and solved it differently.
  • Toolchain, programmer device GW1NR-9C, menu grouping under brisbane_brs_100 through the _pmod_rgblcd_480_272 / _800_480 suffixes.

Theirs is the better plan to assemble. Every peripheral is one unbroken run, the colour bits read in order down J5, and the controls, TM1638, microphone, DAC and spare pins follow down J6. Mine spreads the LCD over both headers, interleaves it with three other modules on J5, and puts B4 above B3 on J5.15–16. My only gain is that every signal sits on the same FPGA pin as tang_nano_9k_lcd_480_272_tm1638, which nobody wiring a BRS-100 benefits from.

Findings against mine

C = claude branch

C1800×480 lab logic samples the 32.4 MHz LCD domain at 27 MHzdefectmine + Tang main

The lcd_800_480 counters run on the PLL's 32.4 MHz output while lab_top runs on the 27 MHz board clock and registers values derived from x/y. The two clocks come from one PLL at a 6:5 ratio, so their closest edges are 6.17 ns apart; a 17 ns path cannot make it.

microphone_monitor, 800×480: slack −10.300 ns, LCD_CK:[R] → CLK:[R], relation 6.173 ns, data delay 17.025 ns

My 800×480 READMEs say “no timing violations”; that holds for the oscilloscope lab only. tang_nano_9k_lcd_800_480_tm1638 on main has the same structure, but its SDC gives the LCD clock a 9 MHz period, so its report cannot show the problem.

C2The pixel clock is declared as an independent clock on an output portriskmine

create_clock … [get_ports {pad_io[9]}] tells Gowin nothing about how that clock relates to the 27 MHz input; the analyser assumes aligned edges. Theirs declares create_generated_clock -source pad_clk_27Mhz -multiply_by 31 -divide_by 27 on the PLL pin, which only works because they keep the PLL instantiated in every lab. I moved to the port after the net constraint failed (TA2003) and a PLL-pin constraint would break labs without graphics.

C3Scattered, interleaved pin plan with B4 above B3usabilitymine

See the pin map above. Twenty LCD wires leave both headers in five separate groups, three other modules sit between them on J5, and J5.15 / J5.16 carry B4 / B3 in reverse order: the likeliest pair to swap during assembly.

C4-cst_warn_to_error 0 silences every constraint error for the boardriskmine

I kept the vendor file byte for byte and demoted CT1108 to a warning in board_specific.tcl. The same switch also hides a mistyped port name or a missing pin. Theirs deletes DRIVE=8 from the five input-capable pins only, keeps the original beside it, and a test pins its SHA-256 and allows exactly fourteen attribute edits.

C5The LED numbering conflict in the vendor documents is not mentioneddocsmine

The BRS-100 pinout PDF lists LED1 on FPGA pin 16; the schematic labels pin 10 as PIN10_IOL15A_LED1. My README and .cst comments present the pinout PDF's order as fact. Their README notes the disagreement and asks the reader to check the silkscreen.

C6Pin data lives in four places with nothing checking themgapmine

The mapping is written into the .cst or top module, the README tables, and both diagram generators, by hand. Theirs keeps one wiring.csv, draws from it, and a test compares it with an independently typed list of the vendor's 32 header pins and with the .cst.

C7Two lab GPIOs and no hackathon branchusabilitymine

Driving INMP441 L/R from the FPGA mirrors Tang Nano 9K but costs a header pin; the module only needs it at ground. My top module also dropped the Tang top's USE_HACKATHON_TOP branch, so hackathon_top labs cannot use these configurations.

Findings against theirs

X = codex branch

X1No configuration builds in Gowin EDA Educationdefecttheirs

board_specific.tcl selects the board's exact part and, by design, has no fallback. The free edition, the one the repository's scripts find here, does not list it, and neither does the 1.9.11.03 Education package installed alongside.

03_synthesize_for_fpga.bash → can't find device: GW1NR-LV9QN88PC7/I6 · while executing "set_device GW1NR-LV9QN88PC7/I6 …"

Students can build none of the four configurations without a licensed Gowin. A Tcl catch that tries C7/I6 and falls back to C6/I5 with a warning works in this Gowin (tested) and would keep their exactness where the part exists.

X2800×480 runs every lab at 31 MHz with 0.4 ns to sparerisktheirs

Moving the lab onto the pixel clock removes my crossing (C1) and keeps clk_mhz an integer, which is clever. The cost is that every lab must now close timing at 31 MHz instead of 27: microphone_monitor reaches 31.79 MHz. A heavier lab, or the TM1638 and audio logic in a bigger design, will fail where Tang Nano 9K's 800×480 passes.

X3TM1638 requires a 5 V module behind a TXS0104E translatorusabilitytheirs

Their README cites the datasheet: TM1638 is specified at 5 V with VIH = 0.7 VDD. But the repository's own tang_nano_9k_simple_setup.png wires the same LED&KEY module to 3.3 V with no translator, and that is how BGM classes run it. Making a translator mandatory adds a part and its wiring that the rest of the project does without.

X4Identifiers used before their declarationportabilitytheirs

pll_locked is read in reset_request before wire pixel_clk, pll_locked;; the 800×480 build also reads pixel_clk early. Gowin, Icarus, Verilator and Yosys accept it; IEEE 1800 does not.

slang: board_specific_top.sv:108:61: error: identifier 'pll_locked' used before its declaration

X5Programmer board name unknown to older openFPGALoaderrisktheirs

-b brs-100-gw1nr9 is listed by openFPGALoader v1.1.1 (oss-cad-suite) but not by the v0.12.1 at /usr/bin here, which is the one the flow finds. Their README says to check. Mine uses -b tangnano9k: same FT2232 channel, known to both versions.

X6Catalog left half-updateddocstheirs

boards/README.md says 55 boards but still ends “Ranges fully measured for 23 of 54 boards”; README_ru.md and README.html are unchanged. The new row breaks the table's vocabulary: no † on an unmeasured toolchain, and “PMOD-RGBLCD (external)” in a column of LCD, VGA, HDMI/DVI.

X7Smaller documentation and interface slipsdocstheirs
  • boards/brisbane_brs_100_pin_mapping.html says “Nothing was pushed”; the branch is on origin.
  • No Gowin GUI fragments: 05_run_gui_for_fpga_synthesis.bash is unsupported (documented).
  • hackathon_top declares gpio [3:0]; their wrapper passes three bits.
  • With TM1638 enabled, lab led[0] lights FPGA pin 16; Tang Nano 9K and mine light pin 10. Without TM1638 all three agree.

Shared, and upstream

S = both branches or main

S1We bet on opposite pixel-clock edges and neither is verifiedopenboth

Theirs changes RGB/DE on the falling edge, assuming the panel samples on the rising one. Mine, like the Tang modules and MuseLab's own demo for this adapter, changes them on the rising edge. The panel datasheet MuseLab ships (GL043056B0, driver NV3047) gives porch limits but no latching edge, and the NV3047 document was not reachable. One design is marginal on real glass.

S2lcd_480_272 gates DE with the pixel clockupstreammine + Tang main

assign LCD_DE = … && PixelClk; drives a combinational, clock-shaped DE off the chip; lcd_800_480 does not. It works on Sipeed's panel; on a different panel over jumper wires it is a candidate for a dark or jittering image. Their brs100_lcd registers DE with the data.

S3Both handle the adapter correctlyclearboth

J2 numbering, colour-bit order, SB1–SB4, R3/R4, power-rail fan-out, PCM5102 SCK to ground and the 5 V backlight path agree between the two branches and with the v1.3 schematic.

What each side should take

Ideas worth adopting

I should take from theirs

  • The contiguous pin plan: LCD on J5.1–16 and J6.1–4 in bit order, then TM1638, microphone, DAC, spares down J6.
  • INMP441 L/R to ground: a third spare GPIO and room for a hackathon branch.
  • PLL always instantiated, create_generated_clock, LCD output delays, a false path on the buttons.
  • Reset held until PLL lock, button release synchronised.
  • Minimal documented edits to the vendor file with the original kept and its hash pinned, instead of a board-wide -cst_warn_to_error 0.
  • wiring.csv as the single source, with a test against an independently typed vendor pin list.
  • Defined outputs when a feature is off; flash chip-select driven high.
  • A wire-by-wire table drawing beside my routing diagram, as an assembly checklist.
  • The LED-numbering caveat and the "J6.1 is GPIO 17, J6.17 is GND" warning.

They should take from mine

  • Build in Gowin Education: C6/I5, or a catch fallback from C7/I6.
  • TM1638 straight on 3.3 V as the default, the translator as an option.
  • Gowin GUI project fragments, so 05_run_gui… works.
  • The pin-1 assembly drawing with its multimeter check of end and column.
  • -b tangnano9k, or a fallback to it when brs-100-gw1nr9 is unknown.
  • All four catalog files updated together, in the table's existing vocabulary.
  • Keep the lab at 27 MHz in 800×480, once the crossing is solved another way.

Open checks

Only hardware answers these

Which DCLK edge does the GL043056B0 latch on?

Run 2_3_color_stripes at 480×272 on each branch. Clean vertical stripes on one and speckled edges on the other settles S1; make the clock polarity a one-line `define on whichever loses.

Does 32.4 MHz (or 31 MHz) survive 15–20 cm jumpers?

Same lab at 800×480, then with a ground wire beside LCD_CLK. Watch for shimmer on colour boundaries.

Does TM1638 work at 3.3 V against the vendor's pull-down on DIO?

A seven-segment and key lab on my _by_claude; if keys misread, try their pull-up edit before a translator.

Which physical LED is LED1?

A lab that lights only led[0]; compare with the pinout PDF and the schematic.

Does the backlight current suit the 800×480 panel?

R2 = 5.1 Ω sets about 20 mA, right for the 4.3" panel's 22.4 V / 20 mA string; read the larger panel's datasheet before powering it.

Recommendation

Next steps, in order

  1. Fix the 800×480 clocking in mine. Either drive the panel from the 27 MHz board clock itself with shorter blanking (one domain, the lab stays at 27 MHz; first confirm the panel's minimum DCLK is at or below 27 MHz), or follow theirs onto an integer-MHz pixel clock and accept the tighter lab timing. Correct the 800×480 README status in either case.
  2. Report the Tang Nano 9K 800×480 SDC upstream: its 9 MHz period hides the same crossing.
  3. Converge both branches on their pin plan, with L/R grounded, and regenerate my drawings from a wiring.csv. This is the largest usability gain on offer.
  4. Adopt the try-C7/I6-else-C6/I5 catch in both branches' board_specific.tcl.
  5. Adopt their clocking and reset scaffolding: PLL always on, generated clocks, reset on lock.
  6. Replace -cst_warn_to_error 0 with their minimal, test-pinned vendor-file edits.
  7. Port test_brs100.py to my configurations, then run the hardware checks above before choosing one branch, or a merge of both, for main.

How this was produced

Both branches were checked out from origin into scratch worktrees. Builds used 03_synthesize_for_fpga.bash with Gowin V1.9.9 Beta-4 Education; worst paths were read from the setup path table in impl/pnr/fpga_project_tr_content.html, not from its per-clock summary. Their configurations were built a second time with only set_device changed to GW1NR-LV9QN88PC6/I5. python3 scripts/tests/test_brs100.py ran on their branch with Icarus Verilog (5 tests, OK). Strict parsing used slang from oss-cad-suite; the Gowin Tcl fallback was tested with gw_sh. The panel datasheet is doc/rgblcd_4.3inch_480x272.pdf in wuxx/icesugar; the BRS-100 pinout and schematic are BrisbaneSilicon's PDFs. The Innolux AT043TN24 link their README cites did not resolve from here.
